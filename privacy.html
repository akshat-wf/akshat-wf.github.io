Privacy policy

WealthFlow · last updated 11 October 2026

WealthFlow is a personal finance app used privately by one household on its own Android phones. This policy says exactly what it reads, where that information is kept, how it is protected, and every way it can leave a phone.

In short

  • Your data stays on your phones. WealthFlow has no server. There is nowhere for your data to be sent, and the developer never receives it.
  • It leaves a phone only when you choose. Syncing with your spouse's phone and passphrase backups are encrypted on the phone first, with a key from your household passphrase. Anything you choose to share yourself — a transfer list on WhatsApp, a backup made without a passphrase — goes as you see it.
  • Google can't read your synced data. Drive sync stores only encrypted files.
  • One optional feature sends shop names to Wikipedia. "Look up merchant categories online" is off unless you turn it on, in Settings › Privacy, on each phone.
  • No ads, no analytics, no tracking, no selling. The app contains no advertising, analytics or crash-reporting code.
  • You stay in control. Every connection can be turned off, and uninstalling removes everything from the phone.

1. What the app reads

  • SMS messages on the phone, to find bank and card alerts: spending, credits, balances. Messages are read on the phone. Each transaction keeps the sender and text of the message it came from, so you can see it on the transaction's page, and a message that looks like a bank alert the app can't understand yet is kept so a later version can read it. Both travel with the rest of your data when you sync or back up with a passphrase.
  • Gmail, read-only (Google scope gmail.readonly). It asks Gmail only for messages from the addresses banks and card companies send alerts and statements from, so the rest of your mail is never seen. It never sends, changes or deletes email. A bank alert's sender, subject and up to 2,000 characters of its text are kept with the transaction it became. Statement PDFs are opened on the phone, using the statement password you save in the app; from a bank account's statement the app also reads the full account number and customer ID it prints, to show on that account's page.
  • The phone's location, only if you turn on "Tag transactions with location" in Settings, on that phone. When a payment arrives by SMS on that phone, or you type one in, the app stores where the phone was: latitude, longitude, how accurate the fix was, and when it was taken. No address or place name — finding one would mean sending the coordinates to a lookup service, so the app doesn't. It uses Android's own location service. Locations travel with the transaction when you sync or back up with a passphrase. "Open in Maps" hands the point to your maps app, which handles it under its own policy.
  • Google Drive, only if you turn on Drive sync. Google's permission for this (drive) allows full access; the app limits itself to two things. To let you pick the sync folder, it lists the names of your Drive folders. After that it opens, writes and deletes only its own wfsync-… files in the folder you picked, and no other file.

From these, the app works out your transactions, accounts, balances, card bills and monthly budget. All of that is computed on the phone.

2. Where your data is kept

  • In the app's private storage on your phone, which Android keeps apart from other apps. Android's device encryption protects it when the phone is off, or has restarted and not been unlocked since; after that, your screen lock and Android's app separation protect it. Your Gmail and Drive sign-ins, saved statement passwords, and full account numbers and customer IDs are stored this way, without further encryption. Tapping Copy beside a number or a bank message puts it on the phone's clipboard, which some keyboards and phones sync elsewhere; the app marks it sensitive so Android hides it from clipboard previews.
  • Never copied by Android. The app opts out of Android's cloud backup and of the "copy apps & data" transfer to a new phone, so neither carries your financial data. To move to a new phone, use the app's own backup.
  • The automatic-sync pairing key is stored encrypted under an Android Keystore key, which never leaves the phone, so a copy of it is useless anywhere else.

3. Every way data leaves a phone

Each of these is something you turn on or do.

  • Directly between your two phones on the same Wi-Fi. All your data is encrypted with a key from your household passphrase; only a short handshake and the phone's name, used so the two phones can find each other, are not. The passphrase itself is never stored and never sent. Automatic sync uses a pairing key made from the passphrase once; it expires after 30 days unless a manual sync renews it. Sync shares everything in the app between your two phones, including saved statement passwords, account numbers, customer IDs and each transaction's message.
  • Through Google Drive, if you turn it on. Each phone writes encrypted change files into a Drive folder the two of you share, and reads the other's. Google stores only ciphertext: it can see that the files exist, their names, sizes and dates, and nothing of what is in them.
  • Backups you make. A passphrase backup is encrypted, includes everything (your Gmail sign-in, saved statement passwords, account numbers and customer IDs too; the passphrase is what protects them), and goes only where you save or send it. A backup made without a passphrase is not encrypted, so anyone holding the file could read it; the app leaves your Gmail sign-in, statement passwords, full account numbers, customer IDs, the text of bank messages (each transaction's, and any it couldn't read) and locations out of it for that reason.
  • Merchant lookup, only if you turn it on. For a shop the app doesn't recognise, the phone asks Wikipedia (run by the Wikimedia Foundation) what kind of business it is. Only the shop's name, as your bank wrote it, is sent — never an amount, date, account, card number, message or your names. A name is sent only when this phone's own purchases show it as a card purchase, and none of its messages show a UPI, IMPS, NEFT or similar transfer; never when it contains your household's names or a title like Mr or Dr. Names can come from bank SMS or bank alert emails. A small shop's name can be its owner's name. Wikipedia sees your phone's internet address and that the request came from WealthFlow, over HTTPS, without you being signed in; its own privacy policy applies. The setting is per phone and covers only that phone's person's (and joint) purchases. Each name is asked about once; the categories found are saved on your transactions and sync to the other phone.
  • Things you share yourself. "Send on WhatsApp" in the budget sends a plain list of transfers — amounts, account names and whose they are — to whoever you pick. A balance enquiry opens your SMS app or dialler with the bank's number ready, for you to send. These leave as you see them, unencrypted.

Reading Gmail and Drive means the app talks to Google's servers on your behalf, signed in as you: your own Google account answering your own phone. The app's fonts are downloaded by Google Play Services. Apart from the merchant lookup above, if you turn it on, nothing else is sent anywhere.

4. How it is encrypted

WhatHow
Wi-Fi sync between the phonesAES-256-GCM; key derived from the household passphrase with PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256, 300,000 iterations
Drive sync filesAES-256-GCM, sealed on the phone with a key from the pairing key; a fresh salt per file; altered files are rejected
Passphrase backupsAES-256-GCM; key from your passphrase with PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256, 300,000 iterations, 128-bit salt
Pairing key on the phoneEncrypted under an Android Keystore key that never leaves the phone
Everything else on the phoneApp-private storage, protected by Android's device encryption

The passphrase is the one secret that protects everything that travels encrypted. The app never stores it; if it is lost, encrypted backups cannot be opened by anyone, including you.

5. What WealthFlow never does

  • It never sends your data to the developer or to any server of its own — there isn't one.
  • It never shows ads, and never uses or shares your data for advertising.
  • It never sells, rents or trades your data.
  • It contains no analytics, tracking or crash-reporting libraries.
  • It never moves money, and never contacts your bank on its own.

6. Google user data

WealthFlow's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Gmail and Drive data is used only to provide the features above, on your own phones. It is not transferred to anyone, not used for advertising, and not read by any person — with one exception you control: if you turn on merchant lookup, a shop's name read from a bank alert email can be sent to Wikipedia, as described in section 3, only to show you which category that purchase belongs to.

7. Your control

  • Turn off Gmail, Drive sync or Wi-Fi sync at any time in the app; the app stops using that access at once.
  • Merchant lookup off stops it at once and deletes the phone's list of names already looked up; categories it already filled stay on your transactions, and you can change them.
  • Location off stops it at once, and can also delete the locations already stored; the other phone deletes them when it next syncs. Any one transaction's location can be removed from its page.
  • Revoke the app's Google permissions altogether at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
  • Delete: uninstalling removes all of the app's data from the phone. Sync files can be deleted from your shared Drive folder, and backups are wherever you saved them.

8. Changes

If what the app does with data changes, this page changes first, with a new date at the top.
