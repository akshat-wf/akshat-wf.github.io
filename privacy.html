Privacy policy

WealthFlow · last updated 9 October 2026

WealthFlow is a personal finance app used privately by one household on its own Android phones. This policy says exactly what it reads, where that information is kept, how it is protected, and the only ways it ever leaves a phone.

In short

  • Your data stays on your phones. WealthFlow has no server. There is nowhere for your data to be sent, and the developer never receives it.
  • Encrypted whenever it travels. The only times data leaves a phone are the ones you turn on — syncing with your spouse's phone and backups — and each is encrypted on the phone before it leaves, with a key derived from your household passphrase.
  • Nobody else can read it. Not the developer, not Google, not your mobile or internet provider. Google stores only encrypted files it cannot open.
  • No ads, no analytics, no tracking, no selling. The app contains no advertising, analytics or crash-reporting code.
  • You stay in control. Every connection can be turned off, and uninstalling removes everything from the phone.

1. What the app reads

  • SMS messages on the phone, to find bank and card alerts: spending, credits, balances. Messages are read on the phone; they are never uploaded.
  • Gmail, read-only (Google scope gmail.readonly), to find bank alerts and card statements. The app never sends, changes or deletes email. Statement PDFs are opened on the phone, using the statement password you save in the app.
  • Google Drive (scope drive), only if you turn on Drive sync, and only to read, write and delete the app's own sync files — named wfsync-… — in the one folder you choose. It does not read anything else in your Drive.

From these, the app works out your transactions, accounts, balances, card bills and monthly budget. All of that is computed on the phone.

2. Where your data is kept

  • In the app's private storage on your phone. Android keeps it apart from every other app, and the phone's own encryption protects it while the phone is locked.
  • Not in Android's cloud backup. The app opts out of Android's automatic backup to Google, so a phone backup never carries your financial data.
  • Sync keys are held in the Android Keystore, the phone's protected key store, so a copied file is useless on any other phone.
  • Screenshots and the recent-apps preview are blocked in the released app, so your figures don't end up in screenshots or on shared screens by accident.

3. The only ways data leaves a phone

Each is something you turn on. Each is encrypted before it leaves.

  • Directly between your two phones on the same Wi-Fi. Everything sent is encrypted with a key derived from your household passphrase; the passphrase itself is never stored and never sent. Automatic sync uses a pairing key, made from the passphrase once, kept in the Keystore; it expires after 30 days unless a manual sync renews it.
  • Through Google Drive, if you turn it on. Each phone writes encrypted change files into a Drive folder the two of you share, and reads the other's. Google stores only ciphertext: it can see that encrypted files exist, their names, sizes and dates, and nothing of what is in them.
  • Backups you make. A backup is encrypted with your passphrase and goes only where you choose to save or send it. You may choose to make one without a passphrase: that file is not encrypted, so anyone holding it could read it, and the app leaves your Gmail sign-in and saved statement passwords out of it for that reason.

Reading Gmail and Drive means the app talks to Google's servers on your behalf, signed in as you. That is your own Google account answering your own phone; nothing is passed on to anyone else.

4. How it is encrypted

WhatHow
Wi-Fi sync between the phonesAES-256-GCM; key derived from the household passphrase with PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256, 300,000 iterations
Drive sync filesAES-256-GCM, sealed on the phone with the pairing key; a fresh salt per file; tampered files are rejected
BackupsAES-256-GCM; key from your passphrase with PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256, 300,000 iterations, 128-bit salt
Pairing key on the phoneEncrypted under an Android Keystore key that never leaves the phone
Data on the phoneApp-private storage, protected by Android's device encryption

The passphrase is the one secret that protects everything that travels. It is never stored by the app; if it is lost, encrypted backups cannot be opened by anyone, including you.

5. What WealthFlow never does

  • It never sends your data to the developer or to any server of its own — there isn't one.
  • It never shows ads, and never uses or shares your data for advertising.
  • It never sells, rents or trades your data.
  • It contains no analytics, tracking or crash-reporting libraries.
  • It never moves money, and never contacts your bank.

6. Google user data

WealthFlow's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Gmail and Drive data is used only to provide the features above, on your own phones. It is not transferred to anyone, not used for advertising, and not read by any person.

7. Your control

  • Turn off Gmail, Drive sync or Wi-Fi sync at any time in the app; access stops at once.
  • Revoke the app's Google access at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
  • Delete: uninstalling removes all of the app's data from the phone. Sync files can be deleted from your shared Drive folder, and backups are wherever you saved them.

8. Changes

If what the app does with data changes, this page changes first, with a new date at the top.
